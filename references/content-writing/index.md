# Content Writing (Indian English, Human Tone)

Use this for any copy that goes into a product: website text, headings, buttons, error messages, emails, docs, and README text. The aim is writing that reads like a thoughtful person wrote it, in clear Indian English, not like a model's default output.

## Voice

- Write in Indian English spelling and usage: colour, centre, organise, programme (for a plan or show), cheque, licence (noun), grey, travelling. Use "lakh" and "crore" with the ₹ sign and Indian digit grouping (₹1,50,000) when the audience is in India.
- Warm, direct, and plain. Talk to one reader as "you". Use "we" for the team.
- Short sentences, mixed with the odd longer one. Everyday words over formal ones: "use", not "utilise"; "help", not "facilitate"; "start", not "commence".
- Be specific. Name the actual thing, number, or outcome instead of a vague benefit.
- Say what the product does in the first line. Do not warm up.
- Avoid stiff, dated phrases ("kindly do the needful", "revert back", "prepone") unless the brand's voice needs them. Do not overdo local slang either; keep it natural and professional.

## Avoid the AI tell-tale signs

Do not use:

- Filler openers and closers: "In today's fast-paced world", "Unlock the power of", "Elevate your", "Seamlessly", "Revolutionise", "Game-changer", "Delve", "Dive in", "Look no further", "In conclusion".
- The pattern "It's not just X, it's Y", and groups of three adjectives or three parallel phrases in every paragraph.
- Em dashes as a habit. Use a full stop or a comma.
- Emoji in headings and bullet points.
- Exclamation marks except where a real person would use one.
- Empty superlatives: "best-in-class", "cutting-edge", "world-class", "powerful", "robust", "innovative".
- Headings that are all Title Case slogans. Use sentence case.

## Make it read human

- Vary sentence length and structure. Start some sentences with the subject, some with the context.
- Put one concrete detail in each section: a real example, a number, a small honest limit ("works with Tally and Zoho Books; QuickBooks is next").
- Admit trade-offs and limits where they matter. Honest copy builds trust.
- Read it aloud. If you would not say it to a colleague, rewrite it.

## By surface

- **Headlines:** under 10 words, one idea, no puns that need explaining. The subheading adds the detail.
- **Buttons and links:** verb plus object ("Create invoice", "Download report"). No "Click here", no "Submit".
- **Errors:** say what happened, why if known, and what to do next. No blame, no jargon, no codes alone.
- **Empty states:** say what will appear here and give the first action.
- **Forms:** labels are plain nouns, help text is one short line, validation messages say how to fix it.
- **Emails and notifications:** the subject says the point; the first line says what the reader must do or know.
- **Docs and READMEs:** goal first, steps numbered, one action per step, copyable commands.

## Placeholder content

Never ship "Lorem ipsum", "Feature One", or "John Doe". Write realistic copy for the actual product, with plausible Indian names, cities, prices, and dates when the audience is Indian. Mark copy the user must approve (legal, pricing, claims) so it is not mistaken for final.

## Before handing over

Check that each page has one clear message, nothing from the AI tell-tale list remains, spelling is consistent Indian English, and numbers, names, and claims are real or clearly marked as sample.
